# PR review speedup: V2-6 paired measurement of quorum-first verification (kata `1yjx`, epic `147f`), 2026-11-11

Nine real review runs were driven through the committed runner (`review-run-cli prepare` / `collect`) on `dev` `7f75b07`, on four PRs: real #207 (head `48c862c`) and three scratch re-creations on this repository (#319, the #205 diff at its measured head `cca69fe`; #314, the #225 diff; #315, the #229 diff). OLD is `dev`'s default (`quorumFirstVerification` unset), NEW is `quorumFirstVerification: true`, the verify concurrency cap is 8 in both arms, and every run is `dryRun: true`: the runner posted nothing. Agents were `tool.task` subagents at effort `hi`, given the runner's briefs verbatim; the harness resolved all 160 of them to `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5` at thinking level `max`. Three PRs have a clean OLD and a clean NEW run (#319, #314, #315); #207 has one OLD run and two NEW runs, both disturbed by jest processes of other worktrees.

The owner's reduced cohort (3 Medium and 3 High PRs, one repeat each, two re-reviews per side, about 16 runs) was not completed: the owner decided to stop and report on the valid runs. Not run: any re-review, #316, #317, a clean NEW run on #207, real #205 at its merged head. Kata `1yjx` (epic `147f`, plan v2 V2-6, `docs/plans/pr-review-speed-plan-v2.md`).

## 0. Summary

- **No wall saving from quorum-first is established, and one clean pair shows the NEW arm much slower.** NEW minus OLD wall, one run per arm: #319 -5.05 min (-12.9%), #315 +3.79 (+13.5%), #314 +9.65 (+51.4%). The first two lie inside the 10.1% to 18.7% spread that identical work showed in earlier sessions (and outside the ramp's cap-8 pair, 24.62 and 25.86 min, 4.9%); the third lies outside it, NEW slower, in a pair with confounds (section 3). The signs differ (mean +2.80 min; the three NEW walls sum to 94.28 min against 85.89 for OLD). One run per arm per PR cannot support a speedup claim, and I make none.
- **The first-wave dispatch saving is real, and second waves spend part of it.** The first wave dispatched 20.0% to 41.7% fewer verifiers than the full electorate would have drawn (the planner's figures for the same rule: 29.4% to 40.7%, `docs/guides/pr-review-run.md`); after second waves the net saving is 33.3%, 27.3%, 27.3%, 16.7% and 0% (runs 3, 2, 4, 9, 8). The share of candidates that needed a second wave was 0, 0, 0.14, 0.60 and 0.57 (runs 2, 3, 4, 8, 9).
- **Every second wave was caused by first-wave verifiers stopped at their 8-minute deadline, never by a split vote.** All second-wave reasons are `missing`; the nine runs recorded 285 votes, 282 `real`, 3 `not-real` (one candidate, unanimous, in OLD run 1) and no abstain, so no NEW run had a not-real or abstain vote. A second wave starts only after the first wave's deadline and added a tail of 6.45, 8.02 and 8.03 min to the verify stage (stage = first wave + tail: 10.75 + 6.45, 8.01 + 8.02, 7.99 + 8.03). In run 8 the second-wave verifier was stopped too, in run 9 two of three.
- **Outcome difference: NEW left candidates unverified in 2 of the 3 clean pairs where OLD left none.** #314: 3 of 5 candidates, one of them Important (f1, 0 votes; a second Important finding on the same line, f2, was verified 2 of 2 and may be the same defect worded differently, section 4.4); #315: 3 of 7 (1 or 2 of the 3 votes needed). OLD left 0 of 6 (#314) and 0 of 6 (#315) unverified, and 0 of 25 on #319. Where both arms reached a verdict on a matched candidate it is the same: of 19 matched, 15 are `present` in both arms, 4 are `unverified` in NEW and `present` in OLD, none differ the other way (a weak check: 282 of 285 votes were `real`, so a candidate verified in both arms was almost certain to be `present` in both, and the matching is a heuristic, section 4.4). An OLD candidate whose verifier was stopped is settled on the majority that arrived (runs 5 and 10: 3 candidates each); a NEW candidate whose first-wave verifier was stopped needs a second wave, which can itself time out (section 4).
- **Reviewer variance is large**, so the arms differ in more than verification: #207 raised 18 candidates (OLD) against 11 and 15 (NEW); #319 25 against 22. Counts of Important findings differ as much (8, 3, 2 on #207; 10 and 4 on #319).
- **Plan targets** (plan v2 section 1.4: Medium at most 25 / 26 min, High first at most 24 / 29, High re-review at most 17 / 21) are not met: Medium median 31.26 (OLD) and 28.41 (NEW, one run disturbed), p75 39.11 and 34.06, in every reading; High first has one PR per arm (28.02 and 31.81 min) and so no statistic; High re-review was not run (section 5).
- **Quality guard** (#207 only): OLD found E1 and E4 (false-negative rate 0.667); NEW run 2 found E1 and E4 (0.667), NEW run 3 found E1 (0.833), both disturbed. There is no clean NEW #207 run, so no per-arm verdict (section 6).
- **`walltime compare` answers `unknown` for every stratum on this cohort** (Medium: baseline 1 PR of the 3 needed; High first: 1 current PR of the 3 needed; High re-review: none). It is not produced as a result; the V2-5 factor view is omitted because High first has one PR per arm (section 7).
- **Incidents** (section 9): all five scratch PRs were closed at 01:55Z on 11-12 through the shared GitHub account, not by the measurement according to the hand-off (the events name only the account; run 7 lost mid-verification); the code under test refuses the `hosted_reuse` key that `dev`'s local-CI config gained in #297 at 01:54Z (a frozen base branch fixed it); a load burst aborted one attempt; the real #205 merged mid-campaign (hence scratch #319). None of these attempts is data.
- **Reading.** Quorum-first as merged shows no established wall saving and a real risk of unverified candidates in these data; keep it default-off. The mechanism is established (a stopped first-wave verifier forces a second wave, which added 6.45, 8.02 and 8.03 min to the verify stage in the three runs that had one, a full deadline where the second-wave verifier was stopped too); the rates, the net effect on wall time, and whether the unverified candidates are more than chance, are not.

A disturbed run is kept, labelled, and never used as the run of its pair; it is excluded from the paired differences. The machine has 18 cores.

- **Every second wave has a stopped first-wave verifier behind it.** Every candidate that entered a second wave had at least one first-wave verifier stopped at its deadline (`secondWaveCauses` in `report-values.json`: 10 of 10 candidates, reason `missing`). Run 4: verifier-9 stopped (candidates f5, f18, f28); run 8: verifiers 1 and 2 both stopped on the same three candidates (f1, f3, f6), then the second-wave verifier-5 was stopped too; run 9: verifiers 6 and 7 stopped, then second-wave verifiers 8 and 9 stopped, verifier-10 returned in 323 s.
- **The stops are not evenly spread.** OLD: 3 of 59 verifiers (5.1%), all in first waves; NEW: 8 of 49 (16.3%): 5 of 44 first-wave verifiers (11.4%; first-wave only against OLD: Fisher exact p = 0.28) and 3 of 5 second-wave verifiers. Over all verifiers NEW against OLD is p = 0.064 (two-sided), over the clean NEW runs p = 0.007; both are descriptive only: verifiers in a run are not independent (the stops cluster in runs 8, 9 and 10), nothing was designed to test it, and the earlier session measured 9 of 111 (8.1%) at the same floor. A second-wave verifier carries the candidates that already cost one verifier its whole deadline; whether that is selection or chance cannot be separated with five.
- **Mechanism, and what it does not say.** With an electorate of 3 (5 at Deep) and `needed` 2 (3), the first wave asks `needed` seats. A candidate is decided once every first-wave verifier of it has been collected (for a stopped verifier: at its deadline); if one of those seats is missing the candidate is a vote short, a second wave is dispatched for the rest of its electorate, and it starts with a fresh deadline: the stage becomes the first wave's deadline plus the second wave's runtime, up to two deadlines. OLD asks every seat in the same round and settles a candidate with a missing seat on the majority that arrived (2 of 3, 3 of 5 `real`), so one stop costs it no second step. For the same per-seat results the two rules reach the same verdict: a candidate ends unverified in either when too many of its seats are missing (2 of 3, 3 of 5). They differ in the time a stop costs, not in which candidates a given set of stops leaves unverified. The candidates NEW left unverified are of that kind (run 8: f1, f3 and f6 had all three seats missing; run 9: f2, f5 and f9 had 3, 3 and 4 of 5 missing), and the OLD runs had none (run 5's three majority-settled candidates each lost one of their 3 seats, run 10's each lost two of their 5: one more stop from unverified). So the open question is whether NEW makes more seats go missing than OLD does (8 of 49 verifiers against 3 of 59; first wave alone 5 of 44, p = 0.28), which three pairs cannot answer.
