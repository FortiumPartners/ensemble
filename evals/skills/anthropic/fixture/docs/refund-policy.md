# Refund and Returns Policy

This policy applies to every customer reply written by the support desk. It is the same for every ticket and changes only through a
reviewed edit of this file. Where a ticket and this policy disagree, the policy wins, and the reply says so politely.

## 1. Scope

1.1 The policy covers physical goods sold through the web shop and the mobile app. It does not cover gift cards, downloadable goods
or services booked through a partner.

1.2 Orders are identified as ORD- followed by four digits. A reply that discusses an order always names it by that identifier.

1.3 A customer may be represented by someone they authorise in writing. Without that authorisation a reply never discloses order
details to a third party.

## 2. Refund window

2.1 A refund is available for 30 days after the order is marked delivered. The day of delivery is day zero.

2.2 For orders marked processing or shipped the window has not started. The customer may cancel a processing order at any time at no
cost. A shipped order is refused at the door or returned after delivery, and the window then starts on delivery.

2.3 Refund window rule 4.2 of the previous policy, the extension for the holiday season, no longer exists. A reply never cites it.

2.4 After the window closes the reply offers store credit equal to the order value less shipping, and explains that this is a
goodwill gesture, not an entitlement.

## 3. Condition of returned goods

3.1 Goods must be unused and in their original packaging. Opened hygiene products, perishable goods and personalised goods cannot be
returned unless they arrived damaged or faulty.

3.2 Goods that arrived damaged or faulty are refunded in full, including the outbound shipping, whatever their condition and for
90 days after delivery. The customer is asked for a photograph, never for the original packaging.

3.3 A reply never accuses the customer of damaging the goods, even when the photograph suggests it. It states what the photograph
shows and asks the customer to confirm how the damage happened.

## 4. How the refund is paid

4.1 A refund goes back to the original payment method. Store credit is issued only when the customer asks for it or when section 2.4
applies.

4.2 Card refunds take five to ten business days to appear after the warehouse records the return. A reply gives this range and never
promises a date.

4.3 A refund to an expired or closed card is paid by bank transfer once the customer supplies the account holder name and the account
details through the secure form. A reply never asks for account details in the ticket itself.

## 5. Shipping costs

5.1 The customer pays the return shipping unless the goods arrived damaged or faulty, or the shop sent the wrong item.

5.2 Return labels are issued from the account page. A reply that includes a label link uses the one in the order record and never
builds one by hand.

## 6. Goodwill credits

6.1 A support agent may grant a goodwill credit of up to 20 euros without approval. A larger credit needs the approval of a team
lead, and the reply says that the request has been passed on, not that it has been granted.

6.2 Goodwill credits are capped at three per customer per year and are never paid out as cash.

## 7. Escalation

7.1 A ticket is escalated when it involves a safety concern, a legal threat, a chargeback in progress, repeated failed deliveries or
a customer who has contacted the desk more than three times about the same order.

7.2 For an escalated ticket the reply acknowledges the situation, explains the next step and names who will contact the customer. It
never closes the matter on its own.

7.3 A safety concern about a product is always forwarded to the quality team the same day, whatever else the ticket asks.

## 8. Tone and content of every reply

8.1 Replies are short, polite and written in plain language. They start from what the customer asked, not from the policy.

8.2 A reply never invents an order status, a delivery date, a tracking number or a refund amount. When the order record has no
answer, the reply says so and states what will happen next.

8.3 A reply never quotes a section number of this policy. It states the rule in its own words.

8.4 A reply never reveals internal notes, team names or tooling, and never mentions that it was drafted with help.

8.5 A reply to a customer who writes in a language other than English is written in that language when the desk supports it and in
English otherwise.

## 9. Fraud and abuse

9.1 More than five refunds in six months, or a refund request for an order that the carrier recorded as delivered with a signature,
is passed to the fraud team. The reply to the customer stays neutral and states that the request is being reviewed.

9.2 A reply never states that a request is suspected to be fraudulent.

## 10. Data protection

10.1 A reply contains only the order details the customer already knows and the status the order record shows.

10.2 A request to delete an account or export personal data is forwarded to the privacy desk and the reply says so.

## 11. Worked examples

11.1 A customer writes on day 12 after delivery that a jacket does not fit. The jacket is unused. The reply offers a full refund to
the original payment method and a return label, and states that return shipping is paid by the customer.

11.2 A customer writes on day 45 after delivery that a kettle stopped heating. The kettle is faulty, so the 90 day period applies.
The reply asks for a photograph and offers a full refund including the outbound shipping.

11.3 A customer writes on day 45 after delivery that a sweater is no longer wanted. The window has closed. The reply offers store
credit under the goodwill rule and explains that it is not an entitlement.

11.4 A customer asks where order ORD-1003 is. The order record says processing. The reply says it has not shipped yet, gives the
expected date from the record and offers to cancel it at no cost.
